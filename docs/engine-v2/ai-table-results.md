## What it shows (2026-11-14)

- **Model seats talk the way we want.** Their messages are short and friendly, name lines, and make concrete offers
  ("I'll keep mine off line 2 if you keep yours off lines 9 and 4"). Deals get accepted.
- **They do not yet beat the naive starter.** All-model tables average 351 points a seat against 398 for all-naive
  tables. In the mix the three kinds tie (380, 382 and 379).
- **No accepted deal was kept by the measure here.** Agents keep moving the named lines until their code is replaced.
  Deals often name four or five lines, so one stray move breaks them.
- **The seats sometimes say their agent stays off a line before replacing it.** The prompt now forbids this; it still
  happens.
- **Honouring a deal is expensive.** An edit costs the program's full summon price, about 400 of the 600 ⚡ starting
  store. That makes the seats reluctant to replace their agent, so only a few of them did.
- **On this table the new starters score less than the naive one** (371 against 398). The harness (24 lines, 1,500 ⚡,
  no refill) showed the reverse (19,236 against 18,285 over 18 seats). Their quarter-of-energy cap trades points for
  ⚡ when the store refills each round.
